The Group’s entertainment center plays classic games through RetroPlayer, the game engine built into Kodi itself — no separate emulator box, no bundled “build” of unknown origin. This page is the audited map of that setup: which emulator cores the center runs, where each core comes from, and where the games it plays may honestly come from.

What this is

An entertainment center is a pile of other people’s software unless someone checks the pieces. Ours was checked. Every component in the center — the Kodi platform, its skin, its streaming add-ons, and the emulator cores on this page — was traced back to its upstream source and admitted only after that audit. Anything whose provenance could not be established was left out, however popular it is.

RetroPlayer turns Kodi into a game machine: load a core for the system you want to play, point it at a game you have the right to play, and it runs — inside the same categorized library as the center’s films, shows, and music. This guide keeps the public record of what was admitted and what was refused.

The emulator cores

A core is the emulator itself, packaged for RetroPlayer. A core enters this table only when its upstream project and license have been verified — the same standard the center’s film and streaming components were held to.

Every core above installs through Kodi’s official repository (Game add-ons → Emulators) and was chosen partly because it needs no BIOS file — the center sources no BIOS files, ever. A core is an emulator only: it ships with no games. On the center’s Linux build, the cores present in the official repository’s Game add-ons category are the ones installed; a core absent there is skipped, never fetched from an unofficial build repository.

Sourcing games — the legal map

Play what you own

The straightforward lane: games you own on cartridge or disc, dumped from your own media, played on the center like the original hardware played them. Ownership is what makes the copy right — the center is a player, and a player is only as honest as what it is fed.

Freely given

A real catalog of classic-style games is simply given away — public-domain releases, homebrew written by its own authors for old hardware, and works whose rights holders released them for free. Those sources are audited like everything else on this page: a source is listed only when its rights status is stated plainly by the source itself.

Source What it offers Rights status
PDRoms An archive of public-domain and homebrew games for classic systems — that is the whole collection, by policy. PD & homebrew only — the archive’s own stated policy

Seeded on day one

The Games library opened with five titles, every one of them verified before it was loaded: 2048 and Mr. Boom — complete games in core form, no ROM file at all; Super Uwol, an NES homebrew game distributed free by its creators, the Mojon Twins; and Tobu Tobu Girl with its Deluxe edition — Game Boy homebrew from Tangram Games, open source (MIT / CC-BY) and given away by the developers.

One caution carried over from the audit: a famous shelf is not a rights clearance. archive.org also hosts bulk uploads of commercial games; hosting is not a license, and those collections are not sources — here or anywhere on this site. The homebrew collections above are approved precisely because they are homebrew by construction.

Not listed — and why

Commercial ROM packs are other people’s property. The big “every game ever” collections circulating online are copies of games whose rights belong to their publishers and creators; downloading them is taking, whatever the download site calls it. They are not listed here, they are not installed on the center, and no page of this firm will point at them.

The center plays what its owner owns, or what was freely given. That is a smaller shelf than the pirate catalog — and every item on it is actually his.

How the center is organized

Five sections

The center keeps the firm’s organizing standard — categorized, tagged, and scannable; libraries, not piles. One home screen, five sections: Movies, TV Shows, Live TV, Music, and Games. Nothing sits loose outside its library.

Movies & TV

Local libraries with proper metadata, scraped and shelved the Kodi-standard way. The starter film library was seeded with public-domain features whose status was verified title by title — the same audit habit as everything else on this page.

Live TV

Free, legitimate, ad-supported channels through add-ons from Kodi’s official repository — Pluto TV is the headline lane — plus the official PVR IPTV Simple Client pointed at iptv-org’s US playlist, a community-maintained open list whose inclusion rule is that the broadcaster itself publishes the stream. The services’ own streams, ads intact, no account tricks. A channel source that cannot be traced does not get plugged in — and any playlist promising premium cable or sports “free” is pirated redistribution, and is refused on sight.

Music

The center’s music library sits in the same catalog discipline — local files, properly tagged, plus open internet-radio directories (Radio-Browser, SomaFM, SHOUTcast) from the official repository. The Group’s own music program — original works, license noted track by track — is published on the Music page.

The Dimon Build

The whole center is a named, reproducible build — Dimon Build v1: a fixed component list, every entry sourced from the official Kodi repository or a verified upstream, exported as a package that can be rebuilt on fresh hardware and come out identical. One skin — Arctic: Zephyr – Reloaded, the modern streaming-service face, with the featherweight Amber skin as the standing fallback — one standard, no wizard, no remote code pulls. If a part cannot be traced, the build does without it.
