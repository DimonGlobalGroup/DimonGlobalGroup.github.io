method

how the record is kept honest
M—01

Built, not assembled

Systems are written line by line on standard-library foundations. If a part can’t be inspected, it doesn’t go in — every component can be read, tested, and answered for.

M—02

Hash-verified releases

Releases ship as packages with published SHA-256 hashes and are regression-tested against their frozen predecessors, byte for byte, before anything is called final.

M—03

Negative results published

Experiments that fail are recorded as failures, in the same document as the wins. A benchmark page that only shows victories isn’t a record — it’s an advertisement.

Public release program

The Reverb Line

The division’s public program runs on a simple rule: publish only what has already been surpassed. Proven generations are released openly — with full documentation — once the in-house line has moved past them, so the public record is always real and the working edge stays in-house.

Under the Reverb banner, retired and abandoned software concepts are also rebuilt from scratch for current technology — clean-room reimplementations, credited to their originals, fused where fusion makes something genuinely new. Releases land on the Group’s GitHub: github.com/DimonGlobalGroup.
